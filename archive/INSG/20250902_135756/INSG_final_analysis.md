Report on Insig AI PLC  

---

**Introduction and Scope**  
This report presents a thorough analysis of Insig AI’s financial condition, ownership structure, operational performance, and valuation metrics as of the fiscal year ending 31 March 2024. The evaluation is grounded in financial statements, key ratios, and ownership disclosures to provide an activist investor lens on the company’s intrinsic value, risks, and operational health.

---

**Financial Performance and Profitability**  
Insig AI is currently in a state of severe financial distress. Revenue has plummeted sharply, from £693,734 in FY23 to £369,860 in FY24—a decline of nearly 47%. Despite maintaining a reported gross margin of 100%, indicating negligible direct costs, this positive gross margin is misleading due to near-zero cost of sales and is vastly outweighed by enormous administrative expenses and impairments.

Operating losses are substantial at £17.6 million, resulting in an operating margin of -567%, and a net loss exceeding £15.9 million. This translates to deeply negative profitability ratios including net margin at -4307.7%, Return on Equity (ROE) at -2031%, and Return on Capital Employed (ROCE) at -157%, all of which signify a company destroying shareholder value at an alarming rate.

---

**Liquidity and Solvency**  
Liquidity ratios paint a dire picture: both current and quick ratios linger at 0.1%, far below healthy thresholds, revealing acute short-term liquidity challenges. The company has insufficient liquid assets to cover foreseeable obligations, signaling potential inability to meet immediate liabilities.

Leverage measures indicate a debt-to-equity ratio of 1.0, which is borderline risky, compounded by an interest coverage ratio of -16.6x, confirming that operational earnings cannot support interest payments, raising a clear financial distress red flag.

Negative free cash flow of approximately £1.3 million reinforces the cash burn situation. Additionally, poor cash conversion and zero EBITDA to free cash flow conversion highlight that operational losses are also translating into cash deficits, not just accounting losses.

---

**Asset Quality and Capital Structure**  
The balance sheet reveals significant impairments totaling around £15 million, prominently reducing intangible assets from £20.3m to £4.4m year-on-year. The tangible book value is negative (-£2.8m), indicating net tangible liabilities exceeding tangible assets. This situation suggests that the company's real asset base is either very weak or impaired, undermining the net asset value.

Capex to depreciation ratio is zero, potentially indicating lack of reinvestment or asset maintenance, which could impair long-term operational capacity.

Goodwill is reported at zero, signifying no intangible goodwill from acquisitions; this aligns with the impairments and indicates limited value derived from acquired assets.

---

**Valuation Metrics and Market Perception**  
Valuation multiples are distorted by the loss-making status and low revenue base. EV/EBIT appears as 0.0x – technically passing the threshold – but this is misleading as EBIT is massively negative, rendering the ratio practically meaningless. EV/Revenue is extraordinarily high at 42x, likely reflecting market illiquidity or speculative premium rather than fundamental performance.

Price to Free Cash Flow and Free Cash Flow Yield measures fail, consistent with the negative cash flow reality.

Overall, valuation metrics do not credibly reflect intrinsic value and appear inflated relative to economic fundamentals.

---

**Earnings Quality**  
Earnings quality indicators show concerning features: an extreme negative accrual ratio (-686.8%) suggests significant non-cash accounting adjustments, likely impairments, which inflate accounting losses. The absence of positive EBITDA to FCF conversion highlights weak cash realization from earnings. The adjusted versus statutory gap is negligible, which confirms accounting figures are not materially restated or manipulated beyond impairments.

---

**Operational Efficiency**  
Inventory turnover at 0.0x signals no inventory movement—consistent with the company’s business model focusing on AI and data services rather than inventory. Days sales outstanding (DSO) is at a reasonable 38 days, indicating decent receivables collection discipline.

---

**Ownership Structure and Governance**  
Ownership is heavily concentrated with insiders and directors, prominently Richard Bernstein holding approximately 18.6% of shares and 1.67 million options. Other directors hold significant stakes, though recent governance changes include resignations and the death of a director, suggesting some instability.

The concentrated insider ownership suggests potential alignment of interests with shareholders but may also reflect risk if liquidity or financing challenges escalate.

---

**Summary and Key Risks**  
- Insig AI is a company in evident financial distress, suffering massive losses disproportionate to minuscule revenues, with severe liquidity constraints and negative cash flows.  
- Operational expenses and impairments wipe out revenue benefits, producing radical negative margins and capital destruction.  
- The balance sheet is weakened by impairments, negative tangible equity, and minimal reinvestment.  
- Valuation multiples are unreliable due to losses and are at odds with fundamental performance.  
- Earnings quality concerns arise from the dominance of non-cash impairments and minimal cash earnings conversion.  
- Governance dynamics indicate insider control but ongoing director turnover that may impact stability.  
- Going concern risks are material, with management signaling the need for further financing to continue operations.  

---

**Conclusion**  
Based on comprehensive analysis of financial ratios, operational metrics, valuation indicators, and ownership information, Insig AI does not currently demonstrate a sustainable earnings profile or a stable financial footing. The company faces substantial challenges in profitability, liquidity, and asset quality, with pronounced cash burn. Market valuation appears inflated relative to economic fundamentals, reflecting either speculative interest or illiquidity effects.

The company’s concentrated insider ownership may play a pivotal role in any strategic restructuring, but current metrics highlight considerable risks. No evidence in the analyzed data suggests a credible imminent turnaround or improved cash flow trajectory. The significant accounting impairments further weaken the asset base and underline structural issues.

Further operational detail, financing plan clarity, and management commentary would be necessary to reassess future prospects. Until such evidence arises, Insig AI remains a distressed entity with limited fundamental value visibility.
