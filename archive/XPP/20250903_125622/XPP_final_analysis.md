Executive summary
XP Power Ltd. displays a highly liquid and broadly dispersed ownership structure, with a free float of approximately 94.9% and no single holder exceeding roughly 11%. Major institutional holders are present, but governance is unlikely to be skewed by a controlling stake. From an activist investor’s lens, the absence of a dominant holder and a broad float supports market-driven pricing discipline, reducing the likelihood of governance distortions stemming from outright ownership concentration. However, the current earnings quality signals present meaningful questions about the sustainability of profitability, which is central to any activist thesis that seeks to unlock embedded value through operational or strategic change rather than pure equity financing or governance leverage.

Rationale and how the analysis was conducted
- Ownership and governance landscape
  - Free float and share class: The single share class with a 94.88% free float indicates high liquidity and efficient price discovery. Company-owned shares are negligible, minimizing the risk of self-holding distortions. This is favorable for price-driven value realization and reduces the likelihood of orange-book manipulation risks linked to concentrated control.
  - Major holders: The largest stake is ~10.84% (Aberforth Partners LLP), with no single holder above ~11%. The top ten holders collectively own roughly 62% of reported equities, implying meaningful institutional involvement but no controlling block. This distribution implies that any activist campaign would need broad shareholder engagement rather than a forced control approach, and it increases the importance of a compelling, data-backed case to win support.

- Earnings quality and sustainability (core for an activist thesis)
  - Revenue dynamics: Six months ended 30 June 2025 show revenue of 110.9m vs. 127.1m in 2024, a material year-over-year top-line decline. Gross margin modestly improved to 41.6% from 40.6%, suggesting some unit economics discipline, but the topline deterioration dominates effect on profitability.
  - Operating profit and adjustments: Operating profit (adjusted) is 2.6m in 2025 vs. 9.1m in 2024. The note indicates that pre-adjustment operating profit was 4.8m, with adjustments of -2.2m, implying non-recurring or restructuring-like charges that depress reported profitability. The heavy adjustments raise earnings quality concerns: unambiguous normalization would require clarity on whether these adjustments are recurring and cash-relevant.
  - Net income and margins: Net loss of 1.8m in 2025 versus a profit of 2.2m in 2024 creates a material swing in profitability. The combination of topline weakness and adjustment-driven profit erosion signals earnings volatility that could be leveraged if a persistent strategy to stabilize revenue and cost structure is presented.
  - Adjustments and earnings normalization: Note 5 details notable adjustments (gross profit +0.2; selling/admin costs -1.7 and -0.7; operating profit -4.4; net effect -3.7). Such material adjustments imply possible earnings management risk unless management clearly distinguishes recurring normalization items from non-recurring charges and provides a robust, cash-oriented reconciliation.
  - Financing and taxation: Net finance expense declined from 5.9m to 4.0m, offering some margin relief. Tax treatment in the period shows unusual patterns; the implied cash tax impact requires additional disclosure for clarity.

- Balance sheet durability and liquidity (critical from an activist stance)
  - The provided balance sheet durability notes indicate attention to leverage, covenant risk, and cash flow conversion. While not explicit in the excerpt, the emphasis on liquidity sufficiency and covenant exposure aligns with a potential opportunity for value creation if operating leverage or asset optimization can be improved.
  - Absence of explicit cash flow statements in the MD files means any assessment of cash conversion quality remains incomplete. For activist decisions, a comprehensive cash flow view would be necessary to confirm the durability of earnings and identify potential liquidity-driven value unlocks.

- Valuation context and mispricing considerations
  - The valuation section notes that a full bottom-up valuation (DCF and multiples) requires a complete set of financial statements, guidance, and current market data. The current data imply a gap between reported earnings quality and price, but without a full cash-flow-backed model and market-based multiples, the precise degree of mispricing cannot be quantified.
  - Given the earnings quality concerns and revenue volatility, any potential mispricing would hinge on the market not fully incorporating the earnings normalization risk or the trajectory for revenue stabilization. The lack of a current, transparent, and robust earnings normalization story makes this a scenario for further diligence rather than an immediate obvious mispricing signal.

Key red flags and observations
- Earnings quality risk: Substantial adjustments affecting profitability (Note 5) raise questions about the sustainability of earnings, especially given the topline decline.
- Revenue resilience uncertainty: The six-month period shows a notable drop in revenue; margin improvement may be insufficient if revenue remains under pressure.
- Ambiguity in EPS reconciliation: The notes indicate ambiguity in mapping adjusted vs. reported EPS to the six-month period, limiting clarity on normalized earnings potential without further disclosure.
- Cash flow visibility: The documents lack a complete cash flow view, which is essential to assess true cash conversion and to evaluate any potential liquidity-driven value catalysts.

Conclusion
- From the information provided, XP Power presents a broadly liquid, widely held equity with no single dominant owner, which is conducive to market-driven pricing and potential shareholder engagement for value-creation initiatives. However, the earnings quality and sustainability indicators in the six months to June 2025 reveal notable weaknesses: revenue decline, a meaningful drop in operating profitability after adjustments, and a sizable gap between reported and adjusted profitability due to notable one-off or non-recurring items. These dynamics imply a fragility in the current earnings base unless revenue stabilizes and cost structures are addressed, which has direct implications for valuation and activist engagement strategies.
- While ownership dispersal supports open-market mechanisms for price discovery, the absence of a full cash flow narrative and forward guidance means a robust intrinsic valuation and a clear path for value unlock remain to be established. Any activist assessment should be grounded in a thorough normalization of earnings (recurring vs non-recurring items), a detailed cash flow model, and a precise plan to address revenue stability and operating leverage.
