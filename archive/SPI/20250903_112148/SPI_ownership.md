## Analysis Summary:

- Executive remuneration is comprised of a substantial fixed element (salary + benefits + retirement), combined with significant variable pay tied to annual bonuses and long-term incentive plans.
- Bonuses are aligned with a mix of financial outcomes (adjusted EBITDA, free cash flow) and strategic non-financial goals, demonstrating a balanced focus on short- and long-term value creation.
- The CEO, Justin Ash, is the highest paid with total 2024 remuneration of £1.81 million, reflecting his leadership role and successful achievement of strategic and financial goals, albeit some metrics fell short of 100% target (e.g., EBITDA bonus portion only about 18.5% of element).
- CFO Harbant Samra's remuneration is lower but still significant with variable pay partly deferred into shares; his ROCE improvement efforts were noted with moderate bonus payout on his individual objectives.
- Former CFO Jitesh Sodha received pro-rated bonus and deferred share awards for the period he was employed as executive director before leaving in May 2024.
- Long term equity incentives (LTIP) form a substantial portion of total remuneration and are strictly performance-conditioned with multiple hurdles, indicating alignment of management interests with shareholders.
- Executive shareholding via LTIP awards and deferred bonuses ensures continued alignment over multiple years and retention incentives.
- The presence of Sharesave plan participation indicates an inclusive approach offering share ownership opportunity to executives consistent with wider employee base practices.
- Overall, remuneration disclosures show transparency and adherence to strong governance principles with clear articulation of performance targets and outcomes.

---

## Conclusion:

The ownership and remuneration data for Spire Health executive directors reveals a well-structured compensation framework combining fixed pay with significant performance-based variable pay linked to financial and strategic outcomes. The data confirms that management incentives are aligned with shareholder value creation through long-term equity awards contingent on rigorous performance metrics including ROCE, TSR relative to FTSE 250 peers, regulatory ratings, and employee engagement.

Despite the company facing some financial and operational challenges as noted elsewhere, the executive remuneration reflects reasonable pay-for-performance principles. The deferred share awards and LTIP retention mechanisms underscore strong emphasis on long-term stewardship and management accountability.

No information on broader ownership such as major shareholders or institutional holdings was provided in the file; ownership focus here is strictly on executive directors.
