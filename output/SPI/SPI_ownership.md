### Analysis Summary

The executive remuneration structure at Spire Health is comprehensive, combining fixed pay, annual bonuses, and long-term incentives aligned with company performance and strategic goals. The CEO, Justin Ash, received a total remuneration of £1.81 million in 2024, down from £2.73 million in 2023, reflecting lower variable pay components. The CFO, Harbant Samra, newly appointed in May 2024, earned £484k, with remuneration reflecting his partial year and prior roles.

The annual bonus scheme is strongly performance-linked, with 60% weighted to adjusted EBITDA, 20% to free cash flow, and 20% to individual strategic objectives. The company achieved EBITDA and free cash flow hurdles, though below maximum targets, resulting in partial bonus vesting (36.1% for CEO, 35.4% for CFO). Strategic objectives were assessed with detailed qualitative progress, reflecting strong leadership focus on digitalisation, quality, and engagement.

The LTIP awards for 2022 vested at 60.82%, reflecting moderate achievement on relative TSR, ROCE, regulatory ratings, and employee engagement. New LTIP awards granted in 2024 have a three-year performance period, emphasizing continued alignment with shareholder value creation.

Outstanding share awards and deferred bonuses indicate significant executive ownership stakes, with holding periods to encourage retention and alignment with long-term company performance. The Sharesave plan participation further supports employee share ownership culture.

### Conclusion

Spire Health’s executive ownership and remuneration framework is well-structured to incentivize performance aligned with financial targets and strategic priorities. The remuneration committee’s transparent disclosure of targets, outcomes, and awards provides clarity on executive pay relative to company performance. The partial vesting of bonuses and LTIP awards reflects a balanced approach to rewarding achievement while maintaining accountability.

The significant share awards and deferred bonuses ensure executives have meaningful skin in the game, supporting alignment with shareholder interests. The remuneration outcomes are consistent with the company’s financial and operational performance as detailed in the financial ratios and statements.

No material concerns arise from the ownership and remuneration disclosures. However, ongoing monitoring of performance against LTIP targets and strategic objectives will be important to ensure sustained value creation.
